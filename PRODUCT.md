# Portfolio product authority

## Purpose

This portfolio helps a recruiter or hiring manager understand Matthew Ralph’s professional positioning, the consequential workflow problems he addresses, the evidence supporting that work, his role relative to implementation agents, the maturity boundaries of each system, and the clearest next step toward a conversation.

It supports business systems, AI transformation, technical-program leadership, governed AI-enabled operations, workflow and decision architecture, AI-system evaluation, and commercial systems thinking. It must not recast Matthew as a software engineer, ML engineer, AI researcher, frontend engineer, or designer-first candidate.

## Audiences and paths

Primary audiences, in order, are recruiter/sourcer, hiring manager, and technically curious evaluator. The site is not optimized primarily for designers, developers, open-source contributors, or social-media audiences.

In the first 30 seconds, a visitor should understand who Matthew is, what kind of work he does, what proof exists, and where to go next. Within two minutes, they should also understand his commercial background, governance mindset, technical implementation literacy, actual role, evidence quality, and maturity boundaries.

Primary proof surfaces are the homepage, the Job Opportunity Engine and Response Flow Systems case studies, focused technical proof (AI Systems Reliability Lab), public work samples/GitHub evidence, and the LinkedIn contact route.

## Product boundaries

The portfolio is not a design showcase, engineering portfolio, public source-code archive, lifestyle brand, startup-marketing site, AI demo gallery, or dashboard product. Desktop and mobile are first-class recruiter surfaces.

Accessibility is a product requirement: semantic structure, keyboard operation, visible focus, descriptive links, meaningful diagram alternatives, measurable contrast, no hover-only critical meaning, and no meaning conveyed only by color.

## Evidence rule

Design may make evidence easier to find, compare, and understand. It may never increase the apparent maturity, scale, deployment status, automation authority, adoption, or production readiness of the underlying evidence. Boundaries, failures, human authority, and current-state labels are part of the product—not disclaimers to hide.
