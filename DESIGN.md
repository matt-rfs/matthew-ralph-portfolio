# Portfolio design authority

## Positioning

**Authored Systems Dossier V002 — Evidence Has Authority**: an editorial publication (about 45%), executive operating brief (about 35%), and technical field manual (about 20%). These are conceptual weights, not layout quotas. The result is calm, authored, deliberate, evidence-oriented, technically credible, commercially literate, contemporary, restrained, and human—not a generic AI startup.

## Principles

1. **Evidence earns hierarchy.** Architecture, decisions, results, validation, and boundaries outrank decoration.
2. **Calm technical credibility.** Precision over spectacle.
3. **Progressive disclosure.** Fast recruiter comprehension; deeper evidence for technical evaluators.
4. **Consistent case-study grammar.** Learn the structure once, then recognize it across systems.
5. **Purposeful density.** Density is acceptable when it communicates substance; whitespace creates hierarchy, not decorative emptiness.
6. **Restrained motion.** Default is none. Motion may communicate state, sequence, hierarchy, relationship, or transition only, and must respect reduced motion.
7. **Mobile is composed, not collapsed.** Reorder, stack, simplify diagrams, and adjust density deliberately; never require a laptop or horizontal scrolling.
8. **Design never outruns evidence.** Visual force must not imply unsupported maturity.
9. **Human judgment is visible.** Important tradeoffs, rejected options, failures, corrections, and authority boundaries must be discoverable.
10. **Human authority is visible, not merely implied.** Where AI or automation participates materially, distinguish system execution, Matthew’s directed decisions, and final human authority.

## Typography and reading

Preserve the existing editorial serif display face (`Georgia`, then `Times New Roman`) for headings, prominent evidence values, and considered statements; preserve the existing Inter/system sans stack for body and navigation. No new font family without a separately approved case.

- H1: display type, approximately 3.2–7.5rem by route, compact line-height; it names the person or system.
- H2: display type, approximately 1.85–4.2rem by page context; it advances the narrative.
- H3: display type only where a named evidence object or decision merits a subheading.
- Body: sans, approximately 1–1.06rem with 1.55–1.7 line-height; reading measures generally remain below 760–820px.
- Lead: larger, quieter sans or restrained display treatment for the single proposition of a section.
- Metadata, evidence, and validation labels: sans, uppercase only when useful, 0.67–0.78rem, tracked and legible; never reduce mobile labels below readable scale.
- Code or technical notation: use only when it is evidence, at local utility scale; do not create a developer-portfolio aesthetic.

## Color and structure

Preserve the existing warm paper, dark ink, muted text, fine rules, restrained green accent, and warm/gold emphasis. These are semantic tools, not decoration:

- ink: primary argument and display text;
- muted: explanatory text and secondary metadata;
- rules: document structure and comparisons;
- green: links, active evidence cues, and calm positive/implemented emphasis;
- warm/gold: attention, human-review, or limited emphasis;
- status: use explicit labels with color as a secondary cue; a failure is evidentiary, never theatrical.

Dark panels are reserved for compact architecture or evidence where contrast improves comprehension. They do not establish a second visual identity.

## Rhythm, surfaces, and diagrams

Use more open narrative spacing on the homepage; purposeful section rhythm on flagship case studies; compact evidence density in focused technical proof; and extra breathing room around diagrams, decision records, comparisons, and validation blocks. Prefer plain ruled sections first. Use an evidence panel, comparison block, validation block, decision record, project card, technical-proof card, or callout only when its structure clarifies a real distinction. Avoid generic card proliferation.

Diagrams answer a recruiter-readable question through clear sequence, restrained node count, visible boundaries, and visible human authority when relevant. Avoid giant implementation schemas and unnecessary notation. Provide a usable mobile fallback, usually by stacking or simplifying the sequence.

## V002 wayfinding and chapter grammar

**Navigation is a map, not a medal stand.** Every substantive recruiter-relevant public destination with its own route appears in primary navigation. Homepage composition—not navigation omission—communicates hierarchy. Desktop may use full names; compact visual labels may be used responsively only when descriptive accessible names remain available.

Homepage chapters have different evidentiary jobs and may therefore use different density, scale, framing, and controlled value fields. Warm paper remains the base; pale evidence green, pale decision parchment, and ink fields may mark a meaningful conceptual transition. Do not alternate surfaces mechanically or use gradients.

The recurring **Authority Ledger** is a ruled editorial annotation, used selectively where it clarifies: **System did / Matthew directed / Human authority remained**. It is not a component-library pattern or a substitute for the surrounding evidence.

Projects retain family resemblance through explicit evidence, Matthew’s role, human authority, maturity, boundary, and public evidence routing. They avoid template monotony through distinct signatures: JOE evidence → state → decision; RFS custody → approval → response assurance; PIR CandidateClaim ≠ Claim; Reliability Lab evaluate → diagnose.

V002 is bolder through hierarchy, typography, contrast, spatial rhythm, and authored evidence framing—not decoration. Each release requires deterministic validation and a subjective rendered critique at desktop, intermediate, and mobile widths; one bounded refinement pass may address issues discovered there before human publication review.

## Case-study and evidence grammar

The recruiter progression is: **purpose/problem → Matthew’s role and decisions → system → authority/decision boundaries → validation or what changed → what it proves → current boundary → inspect evidence**. Literal numbering may vary, but the progression should remain recognizable.

Use an editorial evidence ledger—not a SaaS KPI row—when a project benefits from a rapid proof summary. Its stable fields are **Problem / System / Verified / Boundary**. Desktop may align these horizontally; mobile stacks them.

Use a compact human decision record only for consequential judgment: **Decision / What was chosen / Why / Risk prevented / Validation or consequence**. It is not a replacement for ordinary prose.

Validation/checkpoint treatment is restrained and evidence-led: examples include `CLEAN PUBLIC CHECKPOINT`, `180 / 180`, `VALIDATED`, `PASS`, `HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED`, and `FAIL PRESERVED`. Use only when authority supports the label; never gamify it.

## Mobile and accessibility doctrine

Preserve hierarchy on narrow screens; stack evidence blocks; make technical diagrams vertical when necessary; prevent horizontal page scroll; maintain reachable primary evidence links; retain visible focus and descriptive link purpose; do not depend on hover, landscape orientation, tiny metadata, or color alone.

## Anti-patterns

Do not introduce random gradients, glowing AI blobs, heavy glassmorphism, bento-grid dependence, giant empty heroes, dashboard-card spam, badge or tool-logo walls, terminal cosplay, unexplained icons, gratuitous animation, low-contrast microcopy, independent per-project visual systems, or any visual treatment that implies unsupported production maturity.
